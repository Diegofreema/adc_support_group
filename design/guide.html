Design principle

"One screen, one job"

Taken from the GOV.UK design principles ("Do the hard work to make it simple"), which were made for services that everyone in a country has to use. Every screen follows these eight rules:

One green button per screenThe main thing to do is always the green button. Everything else is quieter.
Plain words"Record an activity", not "Submit entry". "Pay as a group", not "Initiate transaction".
Big targetsButtons and fields at least 48px tall. Text at least 16px (body is 17px).
Icons always have wordsNo icon-only buttons except Back and the bell.
Long forms are broken into stepsA progress bar and "Step 2 of 4". One topic per step. Back is always there.
Status uses words, not just colourEvery status chip has an icon and a word: Verified, Waiting, Rejected, Suspended.
Phone firstDesigned at 390px first. Bottom tabs on phones, side menu on computers. Nothing scrolls sideways.
Say what happens nextBefore payment, removal or rejection, we confirm in a dialog that says exactly what will happen.

Typography

DM Serif Display + DM Sans

A well-known pair from the same family, designed to work together. The serif is used only for moments that should inspire: the landing page, big numbers, and greetings. Everything people read and tap uses DM Sans, which has wide, open letters that stay clear on small, low-quality screens.

Colour

ADC green, white and red

Same green as Rise Up (#1B6B2F). Green carries action, red is only for money moments and danger, and amber means "waiting". Warm cream gives the public pages a printed-poster feel.
